**Pre-call framing:** Lead with the referral angle — you're a senior real estate agent in Bali who works with developers, you see them struggling with marketing and lead gen, and you're looking for someone you can confidently send them to. This is completely true, gets you every reason to ask detailed questions, and makes them want to impress you.

**Questions to ask (in this order):**
1. "If I refer you a developer client, what does the first 30 days look like?" — surfaces their onboarding workflow
2. "How do you keep the developer updated on what's working?" — surfaces their reporting stack and tools
3. "What's the minimum budget you'd want a developer to commit to before you'd take them on?" — surfaces pricing without asking directly
4. "What do you need from the developer's side to get started — do you work with their existing renders or build everything fresh?" — surfaces their creative/visualization process
5. "Where does your work stop? Do you hand off leads to whoever they have on sales, or do you have a closing team?" — **most important question.** Ask it last, casually. If they say "we don't close," that's OLC's natural opening: "Interesting, because I've actually been building something on that side."

**What you're listening for:**
- What reporting tool they use for clients (Looker Studio? Custom dashboard? Manual?)
- Whether they track individual asset engagement (PDF views, landing page by unit)
- Where their handoff point is — and whether that gap is a frustration for them or their clients
- Whether they'd want a trusted closer to refer to (making them a feeder for OLC)

### Campaign Benchmarks — Live Czech Residential Project (June 2026)

Project: Břevnovská Závěrka (Prague residential development), 17 days into campaign.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total ad spend | 27,292 Kč (~$1,200 USD) |
| Form submissions (hard leads) | 76 |
| Soft leads (phone/email clicks) | 9 |
| PDF property card downloads | 2,230 |
| CPA per form | ~$16 USD |
| Best CPA (Demand Gen campaign) | ~$13 USD |
| Google Ads CTR | 4.41% |

Platforms used: Google Ads (PMax, Search Text, Demand Gen, Brand) + Sklik (Czech-specific ad network) + Facebook Ads.

### Geographic Targeting Intelligence

- Bali investment leads (generic "invest from €100K, 10–15% ROI" campaigns) = cheap
- Czech/local residential leads = ~€10–12/lead = expensive
- London high-end buyer targeting = "crazy expensive"
- Eastern European buyers (Hungary, Czech) responded well to €100–200K Bali entry-level products
- Holland did NOT respond to the same product/price point
- Conclusion: targeting geography is less important than buyer profile matching product price and design language. You can run ads from anywhere to anywhere — but the creative and positioning must match the buyer, not just the location.

### Key Tactic — PDF Property Cards as Lead Magnet

His live campaign: 2,230 PDF downloads vs 76 form submissions (29:1 ratio). He tracks each individual property card URL (by unit type, floor plan, or view orientation).

Why it works: a PDF download signals interest before a lead commits to filling a form. Knowing which cards get downloaded most reveals which unit/price/design profile is attracting buyers — before the campaign is over.

**Actionable for OLC:** Recommend individual PDF property cards (one per unit type or floor plan) as a primary lead asset for developer clients. Track each URL in GHL. A unit getting 400 PDF views but zero form submissions is a pricing or design problem — you know before spending more.

### The Visualization-First Principle

Every campaign element must be tailor-made to the project: buyer persona, design language, renders, targeting, price. Using wrong design language for the target buyer (e.g., marble-and-gold renders for a young crowd) wastes the entire ad budget — the traffic works, the creative doesn't convert.

Generic agency failure mode: they don't understand the product or the buyer type, so they apply the same template they used for electronics or hospitality. "You need to be a real estate agent to run effective campaigns."

This validates OLC's Offer Workshop approach: you do not touch marketing until you fully understand the project, the developer's buyer, and the design direction.

### His Lead Offer to Matthieu (OLC Assessment)

- He has ~20K leads from generic Bali investment campaigns (Jan–June 2026)
- Offered for free — commission only on closes
- Assessment: **not worth pursuing.** Leads are generic ("invest in Bali"), not project-specific, up to 5 months old, entry-level investor profile (€100K). OLC closes real-buyer leads on specific projects. Cold generic investor outreach is a different business.

**Model:** Fully integrated end-to-end. Marketing + website + branding + ads + appointment setting + closing. No handoffs.

**Team size:** ~50 people (marketing, closers, appointment setters, creatives)

- No commission mentioned — fixed retainer model

**Performance (live project, ~$32M development):**
- 5 days to rebuild the entire brand (website, presentations, Instagram, all ads)
- 3–4 units/week being closed
- 18 contracts sent in first 15 days
- 12 signatures with deposits in 8 weeks
- Campaigns run on Facebook/Meta (not Instagram directly)
- Target market: Australians exclusively (leverages local network)

**Marketing spend per unit sold:** $2,000–$3,000

**Budget ramp-up strategy:** Start at $15–20K/month, increase to $25K as project nears sellout

**Reporting:** Weekly WhatsApp voice notes from closers + video updates

**Developer involvement:** Only needed for final meeting if client wants to meet them before signing

**Projected timeline:** 100% sold in 3–4 months for Gareth's projects; 6–7 months for full sellout on larger projects

**Key insight:**
- Best-performing agency the developer has worked with. Only one delivering real, measurable results.
- Full accountability — one party responsible for marketing AND closing. No blame shifting.
- Gareth is now pitching co-developer arrangements (10% of project profit) on future deals. He's becoming a partner, not just a vendor.
- This is the Prosper model to beat on positioning.

## Global Benchmarks (Reference)

*Note: these are global rates; Indonesia/Bali rates are likely lower.*

| Model | Global Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Real estate CPL (paid, global) | ~$480/lead | First Page Sage 2026 |
| Real estate CPL (organic, global) | ~$416/lead | |
| Lead gen agency retainer | $2,500–$19,000/mo | Wide range based on scope |
| % of ad spend model | 10–20% of spend | Creates misaligned incentives — avoid |
| Pay-per-lead | $50–$400/lead | Quality issues common |

**What this means for pricing:**
- A developer spending $5,000/month on ads at a 2% close rate from leads needs ~50 qualified leads per close
- At $480/lead × 50 leads = $24,000 in lead gen cost per closed unit (global rates)
- Matthieu's model: developer pays ads + performance fee on close only. If his close rate is significantly higher than 2%, the cost per closed unit drops dramatically.
