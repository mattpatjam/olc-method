## Two Service Models

| Model | What it covers | Pricing |
|---|---|---|
| Agency / Social Media Management | Channel management (Instagram, LinkedIn, etc.) | ~£1,200/mo per channel |
| Fractional CMO | Full-stack strategy: social + ads + PR + influencers + email flows. Outsources execution to specialist partners. | £2-3k/mo base. Full stack (everything): £7-8k/mo + ad budget |

**Ad budget is always separate** from agency fees — client pays whatever goes into the platform directly.

## Team Structure Model

The model that works at scale separates strategy from execution cleanly:

**Strategy layer (western/senior):**
- Sets goals, builds the overarching strategy
- Manages top-of-funnel (PR, influencers, brand-facing outreach)
- Anything that requires western business thinking or western contacts (football teams, luxury PR, B2B outreach)

**Execution layer (local Indonesian staff):**
- Inbound/outbound DMs (Instagram, LinkedIn)
- Graphic design
- Video editing
- Community management (commenting, replying to posts)

**Specialist partners (outsourced per need):**
- Paid ads — outsourced to a dedicated ads agency (BAM Media in Dani's case, UK/Canada-based)
- Email flows — can be Fiverr or a specialist depending on complexity
- Influencer sourcing / PR — outsourced to agencies that specialize in this

## Hiring Local Staff in Bali

**Where to find them:**
- Facebook groups — best results. Search "Bali social media jobs" or "Bali graphic designer."
- Personal recommendations — second best.
- LinkedIn, job boards — don't bother. Too much noise, too many unqualified applicants who apply regardless of fit.

**What locals can handle:**
- DMs and community management
- Graphic design
- Video editing
- Admin and task execution (given a very specific brief)

**What they struggle with:**
- Independent thinking, improvisation, anticipating what's needed
- Working without a detailed brief — they want to please, so they'll wait for exact instructions rather than act
- High-level strategic decisions or brand-facing western business contexts

**How to train them:**
- Start with hyper-specific briefs
- Give them fake test tasks (not real deliverables) to develop independent thinking — see how they handle it without hand-holding
- Give feedback on those tests
- Over months, they develop more autonomy. Dani's best local (graphic designer, ~11 months) now proactively proposes experiments.
- Build internal approval loops: internal revision → internal approval → client approval. Reduces the noise that reaches the client.

## Ads vs. Organic — The Right Balance

**The risk of ads-only:**
- Meta bans happen. Account glitches happen.
- If your entire pipeline depends on ads and the account goes down, cash flow drops to zero.

**The right structure:**
- Use ads to generate traffic
- Capture data off social into an owned email list (earned media) — you own it even if the platform disappears
- Tag subscribers by interest/intent — send targeted email flows
- Email list of 2,000 is worth more than 20,000 Instagram followers you don't own

**Email flows:** newsletters, retargeting, warming up cold leads, educating around the product. Format depends on niche. For B2B/professional services: share expertise, industry updates, credibility content.

## Starting Lean — Her Advice for Early-Stage Operators

- Don't hire a CMO or a full agency before you know what your brand should look like
- Test first — run some Meta ads yourself, put something out, see if there's demand
- Hire one local person to handle the channels (cheap, manageable)
- Figure out what you actually want before locking someone in to build it for you
- Don't over-plan. A rigid plan built too early creates stress and usually gets thrown out anyway.
- Once you see what's working and what you want more of — then bring in specialist help
