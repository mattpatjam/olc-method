# Brand Foundation: OLC Method
*Built: 2026-06-16*

---

## 1. Secret Formula

- **Dream customer:** Experienced C-suite decision-maker, founder, or developer at a real estate development company or established hospitality brand. The project is concept-driven, fully managed, amenity-rich, and ideally fully funded. Not a first-timer. Has developed projects before and knows the difference between a vendor and a real partner. Age, gender, and location are secondary. Starting market: Bali and surrounding islands. Expanding to Southeast Asia.

- **Where they are now:** They have a strong, differentiated project and no sales system that matches its quality. Local agencies treat it like any other listing, drag the price down, and can't communicate the value to the right buyers. Marketing budget gets spent on funnels built for retail villa buyers, not concept-driven investment projects. The project is sitting there, partly sold or barely launched, while the developer watches the market flood with look-alike units that have nothing in common with what they've built.

- **What attracts them (bait):**
  - "Tired of agencies dragging your price down and comparing your project to any other listing on the market?"
  - "Wasting budget on funnels that were never built for a value-driven branded concept?"
  - "Looking for a fully integrated sales system with real international exposure?"
  - "Tired of wasting time and money on marketing that isn't designed for your type of project?"

- **Result we deliver:** Every unit sold, faster and more efficiently than through any local agency. A lead feedback report at the end of the engagement — built from AI sales call transcripts, capturing what worked and what didn't — so the developer can make their next project even stronger. Capital freed and ready for the next deal. A professional partner from the first conversation to the last closing, who didn't disappear when things got hard.

## 2. Mission Statement

> "OLC is a sales and marketing partner for concept-driven real estate and hospitality projects. We design the offer, build the demand, and close the deals. The developer runs their project; we run their sales engine."

## 3. Value Ladder

| Rung | Offer | Price | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | Free project audit + value-driven offer creation | Free | Any qualified developer — first contact, zero commitment |
| Onboarding | Full strategy build: playbook, ICP profile, offer document, 90-day roadmap | $10K-$20K (avg $15K) | Developers committing to the full engagement |
| Core engagement | Performance partnership: marketing management, lead generation, closing | 5% per close (or 2.5% leads only) + client pays ad spend | Projects with $2M+ total inventory |
| Add-on | Sales training for developer's internal team: audit, 5 sessions, strategy session with leadership (~30hrs) | $180/hr (approx. $5,400 total) | Developers with their own closing team |
| Exit bonus | Lead feedback report: AI transcript analysis of every sales call, what worked and what didn't | Free | All clients at end of engagement |
| Private | Profit sharing on next project | Negotiated case by case | Long-term partners only — not marketed publicly |

**Gaps identified:** No intermediate rung between the free audit and the $15K onboarding. A developer who's interested but not ready to commit has no place to go except yes or no. A low-ticket diagnostic or paid strategy session ($500-$2K) could bridge this gap in a future iteration.

## 4. Attractive Character

- **Type:** Leader / Guide. Matthieu has done this — spent five years closing premium real estate deals personally, sold approximately 50-100 villas in Bali, and built the system because he saw the gap no one else was filling. He's not figuring this out alongside anyone. In Expert Secrets terms: the guide archetype. Developers are the heroes — they built the project, they are on the journey. OLC is the mentor who gives them the new opportunity (the performance sales partnership) and the framework to reach the result. OLC never positions itself as the hero of the story.

- **Backstory:** Saw premium, concept-driven projects going unsold in the Bali market because local agencies couldn't communicate their value. Had the skills to sell them himself. Watched the same pattern repeat — strong project, wrong sales partner, slow results. Took matters into his own hands and built OLC to fix a problem the industry wasn't addressing.

- **Character flaws:** Has been under the sun chasing unqualified leads, spending hours on fruitless viewings, feeling the frustration of wasted time and wasted hope. He knows what it feels like to get your hopes up and walk away with nothing. He built a system partly because he got burned by the same broken process he was working inside.

- **Polarity:** The Bali real estate market is maturing. Buyers are more international, more sophisticated, and less interested in undifferentiated product. The shoebox villa era is running out of road — too many of them, no competitive advantage, and increasingly risky from a legal and construction standpoint. Small developers often lack proper permits, use the wrong materials, and operate in legal grey areas. Smart investors have started to notice. The real opportunity is in branded, community-driven, service-oriented projects with real management behind them. Most agents in Bali won't say this because they're still making commission on the other kind.

- **Opportunity switch (OLC's new vehicle):**
  - Old vehicle: local agencies, scattered brokers, ad-hoc commission structure
  - Why it fails: agencies list the project, don't own the result, can't communicate concept value, compete on price, disappear after the first viewing, no accountability
  - What the developer feels: powerless, undersold, frustrated, like they built something great that's being misrepresented
  - New vehicle: outsourced sales partner — one partner designs the offer, builds demand, and closes deals. Paid only when units close. Not a better agency. A different category.
  - OLC cannot be described as "a better version of a local agency." It is a performance-based sales partnership. If it ever sounds like an improvement offer, the framing needs to be rebuilt.

- **Status play (what working with OLC does to a developer's self-image):**
  - Intelligence: "I chose the smart, non-obvious partner — not just whoever showed up"
  - Wealth/power: "I freed capital faster and moved to the next project while others were still waiting on slow agents"
  - Style: "I operate at a level where I have a real sales system, not just a listing on a portal"
  - Status protectors built into the OLC offer: done-for-you execution (developer approves, OLC does), 90-day money-back guarantee, real-time campaign dashboard, exclusive lead clause

- **Cause:** Building something of his own. No more working for someone else. Financial independence and professional ascension through becoming a genuine partner in larger branded projects. Access to a different league of people. The ability to work from anywhere in the world without being tied to one location.

- **Primary story arcs:**
  - Us vs them: against the local agency model and the commoditized villa market
  - Secret telling: the market is shifting and most developers don't have a system that can keep up
  - Third-person testimonial: client results (starting with Nirvana) tell the story better than any pitch

## 5. Hook / Story / Offer Inputs

- **Traffic temperature:** Warm for Bali-based contacts and LinkedIn followers. Cold for anyone outside that network. OLC has no brand recognition yet — Matthieu's personal reputation carries the weight for now.

- **Awareness level:** Between problem aware and solution aware. They know their current setup isn't working. Some have started looking for alternatives. Most haven't found the right one yet.

- **Hook angle:** The wasted money and wrong partner problem. Developers are paying people who aren't moving inventory and know it. The hook that lands is the one that names that frustration out loud: "Tired of wasting money on marketing and sales partners that aren't selling your project?"

- **Core story (1 sentence):** A developer with a premium project and no reliable sales partner in an unstructured market finds OLC and walks away with every unit sold, a professional system, and a partner they'll call again for the next one.

- **Offer framing (1 sentence):** You sell your concept-driven project without wasting budget on agencies that treat it like any other listing.

## 7. The Big Domino

The entire OLC pitch is organized around one belief. If a developer walks away holding this belief, every other concern dissolves.

> "A performance-based sales partnership is the only way to sell a premium project without being undersold — and it's only available through a partner who designs the offer, builds the demand, and closes personally."

Every developer conversation, LinkedIn post, proposal, and intro call points back to this. When an objection surfaces (price, trust, timing, market), the response is not a counter-argument. It's a return to the domino.

**OLC's Story Inventory** — the 4 stories Matthieu tells to break developer false beliefs:

| Story type | False belief it breaks | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Origin story | "Sales partners don't work — agencies have always let me down" | [NEEDS WRITING] |
| Vehicle story | "I don't understand how a performance model actually works in practice" | [NEEDS WRITING] |
| Internal belief | "My project is too niche / too specific for an outside partner to sell it" | [NEEDS WRITING] |
| External belief | "The Bali market is too slow right now / the timing isn't right" | [NEEDS WRITING] |

Each story: 60 seconds, plain language, ends at the moment of belief change. Built from Matthieu's real client history.
