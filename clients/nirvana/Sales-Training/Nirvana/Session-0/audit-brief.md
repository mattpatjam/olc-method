# Nirvana Sales Audit — Brief

*Audit date: June 9, 2026*
*Prepared by: Matthieu Jammers*

---

## What's Working

- Team structure is sound: Natasha qualifies and manages CRM, Susi closes. Natural split that matches their DISC profiles. Don't break this.
- 18/32 units sold, 1 pending, 4 in negotiation. Strong for a first-time developer with no track record.
- Susi's brand storytelling lands. The "700 people a day, community, retreat program, people stay all day" narrative resonates — confirmed live on a prospect call.
- Objection handling on price (ROI math, brand quality) and track record (contractor portfolio, Kempinski precedent) is instinctively solid.
- Organic referrals are happening without a system. Buyers are happy and referring spontaneously.
- Payment structure — especially the 10% construction interest for full upfront — is a strong differentiator that Susi uses well.

---

## What's Broken

**1. No discovery before pitching (highest impact)**

Susi goes straight to presentation after two minutes of rapport. She never asks: what's your timeline, what's your budget range, what else are you comparing this to. She gets this information only when buyers volunteer it. Works with engaged, forthcoming leads. Fails silently with everyone else — she never knows why.

**2. No committed next step at the end of every interaction**

Calls and meetings close with "I'll send you the details" or "drop me a message if you have questions." No scheduled follow-up, no confirmed date, no concrete ask. This is where leads go cold. The client has no reason to re-engage, and Susi has no hook to follow up without feeling pushy.

**3. Product knowledge gaps**

The team cannot answer basic construction questions on the spot — AC system type, water filtration specs, warranty scope, club access logistics, PT company details. For a $189K–$259K investment decision, every "I'll check and come back to you" is friction. Buyers feel the hesitation. It erodes trust in the developer's credibility.

## Secondary Issues

## Follow-Up Sequences — Marketing-Controlled

**The sales team (Susi and Natasha) have no control over these sequences.** They are created and sent by the marketing team via HubSpot. Raising them is a Toby conversation, not a training item.

The team runs two parallel sequences: 4 emails and 5 WhatsApp messages, triggered when a lead comes in through the website or social media. Both sequences share the same fundamental problems.

**Identity confusion from the first message**

The first WhatsApp message is signed "I'm Susi, Sales Manager" but is sent from Maria Natasha's HubSpot profile. Before the conversation has even started, the lead is already confused about who they're talking to.

**All pitch, zero discovery**

Across 9 messages total — 4 emails and 5 WhatsApp — not one question is asked about the prospect's timeline, budget, or investment goals. Every message assumes the same thing: the person needs to be convinced. Works with the small percentage who are already sold. Fails everyone else silently.

**ROI numbers presented without distinction — credibility risk**

The guarantee and the projection are two different things, but are being presented as interchangeable:
- 10% net minimum = contractual guarantee, first 2 years of operations
- Up to 15% net = projection based on current demand and occupancy (not guaranteed)

Both numbers are defensible. The problem is they appear across channels without context, making it look like three inconsistent claims rather than a layered value proposition. Any serious investor who compares messages will raise it as a credibility question. The fix: lead with the guarantee, follow with the projection and the evidence behind it ("10% guaranteed for the first 2 years. Based on current demand, projections go up to 15% net.").

**Wasted touchpoints**

The construction update WhatsApp message reads: "Key structural areas are actively under construction, with steady progress across the site." This is filler. A specific milestone — "top floor concrete poured, 6 weeks ahead of schedule" — is a trust-building moment. The generic version is noise.

WhatsApp messages 4 and 5 are near-identical to message 1. Zero memory of prior contact. Any lead who received all 5 knows they're in a mass blast.

**The breakup email surrenders**

The final email says: "We completely understand if now isn't the right time… reconnect anytime." No urgency, no hook, no reason to act. It hands the decision entirely to the prospect and expects them to self-motivate. They won't.

**No committed next step anywhere**

No message in either sequence ends with a specific date, time, or reason to act now. "Short call," "this week," "whenever you're ready" are not next steps. They are invitations to procrastinate.

See `follow-up-sequences-rewrite.md` for rewritten versions of both sequences.

## Recommended Training Focus

**Priority 1 — Discovery first:** Three questions before any pitch. What's your timeline for making a decision? Do you have a rough budget in mind? What else are you looking at? Non-negotiable. No presentation starts without answers to these.

**Priority 2 — The committed close:** Every interaction — call, site visit, WhatsApp exchange — ends with a scheduled next step. Not "let me know." A date. A time. A reason to talk again.

**Priority 3 — Product mastery:** Both salespeople must answer any construction or operational question instantly and with confidence. Not "I'll check" — an answer, or a clean redirect to the right expert with a timeline.

---

## Quick Win Before Session 1

Build a product knowledge cheat sheet: every construction spec, every FAQ answer, every objection response, written out. Susi and Natasha study it before the first session. No training required — just preparation. This alone removes one of the three biggest trust gaps.
