# Marketing Team Meeting — Internal Prep
*Nirvana Life | Next week | Internal use only*

---

## Your 60-Second Intro

"I've been working with Susi and Natasha for the past six weeks on the sales side — an audit of the pipeline, then two workshop sessions to build out the offer stack and objection framework. What I found is that there are three distinct buyer profiles, each with a different reason to buy and a different blocker. Today I wanted to share that work so we're all describing the same opportunity in the same language — and figure out if there's a warm outreach channel we can run alongside the paid campaigns."

Keep it to that. Don't pitch. Don't position it as consulting. You're a peer in the room.

---

## Meeting Flow

1. **Introduce the brief** (5 min) — hand it over, walk through page 1 quickly (the sales work context). This earns you credibility before you say anything about marketing.
2. **Walk the three buyer profiles** (10 min) — page 4 of the brief. Ask the marketing team: which of these is your ads currently targeting? Who's converting? This invites collaboration, not lecture.
3. **Show the gap** (5 min) — pages 2-3. The broken [X]% on the website is the one to flag explicitly. If Toby doesn't already know, he needs to.
4. **Propose the two-channel split** (5 min) — page 6. Stress that their campaigns and their sales team pipeline are untouched. Channel B is additive.
5. **Make the ask** — see below.

## The One Ask from Toby

If Toby asks about the commission structure — point to the existing proposal. 5% on OLC-sourced leads. Already in writing.

---

## What NOT to Do

- Don't mention any fee or invoice for the marketing consulting work. The commission is the payment. Don't create confusion about what's charged and what isn't.
- Don't position OLC Method as a competing agency or suggest you're replacing what the marketing team does. You're augmenting, not competing.
- Don't get drawn into a conversation about ad budgets, platforms, or campaign mechanics — that's their domain. Your value is the message, the story, and the warm channel.
- Don't bring up Susi or Natasha's individual performance by name. Refer to "the team" if it comes up.
- Don't over-promise on the email/member outreach results. "Let's run one test sequence and see what converts" is the right frame.

## Optional: Ad Creative Talking Points (If the Room Opens Up)

Don't lead with these. Use them only if the marketing team raises ad performance, creative, or lead volume. They position you as someone who understands the full funnel, not just sales. Each one is 60 seconds max.

**1. The market pyramid (why the member list is strategic, not just tactical)**

"Most property campaigns are fighting for the 3% of the market who are ready to buy right now. The other 97% — people gathering information, problem-aware but not searching yet, or lifestyle buyers who haven't connected Nirvana to the idea of ownership — are invisible to a 'Book a viewing' campaign. The member list is how you reach the 7% and 12% with something valuable before they're ready to buy. It's not a sales channel. It's a warm education channel."

Use this to frame WHY Channel B matters strategically, not just as another sales tactic.

**2. The High-Value Dobie test (if they show you current ad creative)**

The winning ad formula: intrigue around the HOW + specific targeted benefit. Neither alone works.

- Doesn't work: "The Sanctuary by Nirvana Life — luxury suites from $189K. Book a viewing."
- Works: "How Bali wellness investors earn guaranteed 10% income without the offshore complexity — here's the structure."

The second version hides the mechanism (creates curiosity), names the benefit specifically (qualifies the reader), and doesn't show the price upfront (removes the filter before the hook lands). If they show you current ads that look like option 1, this is a 60-second win you can hand them on the spot.

**3. The identity trigger (immediately actionable for their next campaign)**

Take their best-performing ad and add a niche identity word to the headline. Meta's algorithm reads creative to find audiences — the identity keyword signals which pocket to target.

- Generic: "How investors earn guaranteed 10% annual yield from Bali real estate"
- Niched: "How European investors earn guaranteed 10% annual yield from Bali wellness real estate"
- Niched: "How Singapore-based expats earn guaranteed 10% from Bali wellness real estate"

Same ad, different identity signal. Suggest they test one niched version alongside their control. Results in 5-7 days. CPL typically drops because the audience match improves.
