1
Dream Outcome Mapping
25 minutes

Before we sell anything, we need to know who we're actually selling to and what they're really buying. Not the apartment. The life. The rule: sell the outcome, not the property.

Every buyer runs one check before saying yes: will this make me feel better or worse about myself? Not better to others. How they see themselves. The Pure Investor who buys wants to feel like someone who makes smart, deliberate moves with their money. The Lifestyle Investor wants to feel like someone who built a life that actually works. The Nirvana Member wants to feel like they belong here for real, not just on a visitor pass. A pitch lands when it speaks to that internal story first.

The 3 buyer profiles
A Pure Investor. Motivated by ROI, yield, and capital growth. The wellness story is secondary. Needs the numbers to stack up before anything else matters.
B Lifestyle Investor. Wants a Bali base and passive income. Drawn to wellness as a genuine part of the decision. Often discovers Nirvana through the investment, not the other way around.
C Nirvana Member. Already obsessed with the club. Travels to Bali specifically to come here. Experiences the accommodation gap on every visit. Not a cold investor. Converting from believer to owner.
1. Who is this person and what matters most to them?
2. What does their life look like 3 years after buying at Nirvana?
Sales tool: ask the buyer this directly. "Three years from today, what has to have happened for you to feel happy about where you are?" Let them answer. Build the rest of the conversation around what they say.
3. Most common buyer today?
4. Most valuable buyer?
5. Are they the same person?
6. Where do you find them, before they walk through the door?

2
Value Equation Audit
20 minutes

Every purchase decision comes down to four levers. Score The Sanctuary on each from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong). Be honest. The gaps are more useful than high scores.

Lever What it means for Nirvana buyers Score (1–5)
Dream Outcome Does our offer communicate the life the buyer gets, not just the property?
Perceived Likelihood How confident is a buyer that the ROI, management, and lifestyle promise will actually deliver?
Time Delay How long before they see real benefit? Is the path to return clear and credible?
e.g. "We say Q4 2026 handover, but buyers don't know when rental income actually starts."
Effort & Sacrifice How much complexity, risk, and friction does the buyer take on by saying yes?
e.g. "Buyer says yes in person, then goes home and has to manage a $190K foreign investment with no guide."
What the scores tell you

Your highest score is your opening move. Lead with it. Your lowest score is where buyers fall off. Circle it above. The Offer Stack in Step 4 is built entirely to fix that one lever. If two scores feel equal, pick the one that shows up most in deals you've lost.

For most real estate projects, the weakest lever is Perceived Likelihood. Buyers want the outcome. They just don't fully believe it will happen for them. If yours is different, name it clearly. That gap shapes every decision in Step 4.

Build the Offer Stack
30 minutes

For each major objection from Step 3, we build a solution. Not a rebuttal. Something concrete that removes the fear before the buyer even raises it. Then we name it and give it a value.
