## 1. Who exactly are we selling to?

**Primary buyer: Profile C, The Nirvana True Believer**
- Already obsessed with the club. Travels to Bali specifically to come here, not the other way around.
- Spends all day at Nirvana, then sleeps somewhere that doesn't match the experience.
- Has $190K+ in investable capital. Smart enough to understand a guaranteed return.
- Not a cold investor. Doesn't need convincing about Nirvana. They already believe.
- Every visit, they face the same problem: the club is premium, their accommodation isn't.

**Secondary buyers:**
- Profile B (Lifestyle Investor): Wants a Bali base and income, drawn to wellness. Discovers Nirvana through the investment, not the other way around.
- Profile A (Pure Investor): ROI-first. The wellness story is secondary. Needs the numbers to stack up.

**Key insight to surface in Step 1:**
The most overlooked buyer is Profile C. They are already inside the building. Susi and Natasha don't need to find them. They need to ask them.

---

## 2. What matters to this person?

**In order of priority:**

1. **Community:** they love the Nirvana circle. Knowing their people will be there every time they arrive.
2. **Certainty:** Bali accommodation is unpredictable. They want to know exactly what they're getting.
3. **Belonging:** owning a piece of Nirvana means they're not just a visitor, they're part of it permanently.
4. **Lifetime membership:** the hook. Already paying for access or paying per visit. Owning makes that permanent.
5. **Passive income:** the 10% guaranteed return makes the lifestyle choice financially rational.
6. **Zero hassle:** fully managed. They don't want to deal with operations from abroad.

**What does NOT matter first:**
- Square footage (they're not buying space, they're buying access and income)
- Pool (wrong benchmark; see mis-comparing buyer below)
- Views (nice to have, not the reason they buy)

**The mis-comparing buyer:**
Has heard about The Sanctuary but is comparing a 40sqm suite to a 3-bed villa with a pool. The reframe: "You're not comparing the same product. A villa at $400K has no guaranteed yield, no management, no community, no club access. A suite at $189K has all of it. You're not buying square footage. You're buying a performing home base inside the best wellness community in Canggu."

---

## 3. What are the objections?

**At first contact:**
- "Too expensive" → not until they understand the yield and membership stack
- "This is your first project. How do I know the quality?" → contractor portfolio, Kempinski precedent, 18/32 sold
- "I have no privacy, I want my own space" → suite is fully private, wellness-designed, soundproofed
- "It'll be too noisy next to the gym" → soundproofing spec and sleep science design
- "I won't experience the real Bali" → 28 nights/year is a starting point, not a limit. They can leave.
- "The remaining units have no sauna and no views" → Tower 1 is not lesser Tower 2; it has its own positioning (see below)

**In the 48 hours after:**
- "How do I verify the 10% is real?" → it's contractual for the first 2 years, written into the management agreement
- "What if Nirvana fails?" → honest answer: 10% guaranteed covers the first 2 years; after that it's performance-based
- "Is a 27-year leasehold safe?" → PBG permit confirmed, extension clause in lease, standard Bali structure
- "What if construction never finishes?" → construction ahead of schedule, specific milestones (get these from team)
- "I need my advisor to review first" → give them the legal summary document (does one exist? Ask the team.)
- "What if I need my money back?" → $10K refundable within 14 days

**At commitment:**
- "I want to wait for more progress" → cost of waiting: units disappearing, same price pays less later
- "What if I pick the wrong unit?" → team to answer: what's the right unit for each profile?

**Tower 1 reframe (for remaining units):**
The ground floor units with no sauna/view are NOT lesser Tower 2. They are The Investor's Entry Point: lowest price, same guaranteed yield, same lifetime membership, same management. For a pure yield buyer: why pay more for a view you won't be there to see?

## 4. How do we answer each objection?

**The structure that works (Chris Voss):**
Label first → validate the concern → pivot to the proof.

"It sounds like you're not sure the numbers are real." (label)
"That makes sense. A lot of Bali projects make claims they can't back up." (validate)
"Here's the difference: our 10% is contractual, not projected. It's written into the management agreement. On top of that, 15% is what we're actually projecting based on current demand." (proof)

**Key proof points to have ready (memorized, not read):**
- 18 of 32 units sold (56% sell-through on an off-plan project with no track record)
- 10% guaranteed = contractual minimum, first 2 years, written into the management agreement
- 15% = conservative projection based on current occupancy and club demand
- Construction ahead of schedule (specific milestone. Ask team for the exact update.)
- PBG permit confirmed
- Contractor portfolio: has delivered SAOTA and Budipradono projects in Bali (Kempinski precedent. Confirm framing with Toby before using.)
- $10K fully refundable within 14 days. Lowest barrier to entry to reserve.
- Lifetime Nirvana Life Club membership included
- 28 nights/year personal use

**The mis-comparison reframe (most common lost sale):**
"I understand you're comparing it to a villa. But a villa at that price gives you size and a pool, and it doesn't give you a guaranteed return, a management team, lifetime club access, or a community. The comparison isn't villa vs. suite. It's: do you want a property that's also a performing asset, or a property that's just a property?"

## 6. What should the Offer Stack look like?

**Target for Step 4:** every element named, valued, and solving a specific objection.

Guide the team toward this structure. These are the elements they should arrive at:

| Element | What the buyer gets | Objection it addresses |
|---|---|---|
| The suite | 40-44sqm, fully furnished, wellness-designed, soundproofed | "I want my own space" |
| Yield guarantee | 10% contractual minimum, first 2 years, written into the management agreement | "How do I know the ROI is real?" |
| Lifetime membership | Full Nirvana Life Club access, for life, from day of signing | "Will I belong here? Will I miss Bali?" |
| 28 personal use nights | Suite reserved for the owner, prioritized, every year | "I want to actually use this property" |
| Fully managed | Zero operations for the owner. Nirvana handles everything. | "I can't run a rental from abroad" |
| Pre-booked retreat occupancy | Retreat operators pre-book units annually, guaranteeing base occupancy | "What if it sits empty and I don't hit 10%?" |
| Legal support package | PBG permit, leasehold documentation, extension clause. Available for lawyer review. | "I need my advisor to sign off" |
| $10K refundable reservation | Lock in your unit, fully refundable within 14 days | "I'm not ready to commit $189K right now" |

**Push them on standalone values.** If the team hasn't thought about this, ask: "What would a hotel charge per night for a Nirvana suite? Multiply by 28." Total stack should read as 5-10x the asking price.

**Offer name to push toward:** Current name ("The Nirvana Lifestyle Investment") is generic. Better options to float: "The Sanctuary Owner Program" or "The Nirvana Home Base." Let the team feel it out.

## 7. The Final Offer (Step 5 — last 15 minutes)

The session ends here. Three things to walk out with: a name, a price anchor, and one sentence. If you only get one, get the sentence.

---

### Offer name

The current name ("The Nirvana Lifestyle Investment") is generic. It could be any project in Bali. The name should make a Nirvana regular feel it was built for them specifically.

**Options to float if the team is stuck:**
- "The Sanctuary Owner Program" — clear, ownership-forward
- "The Nirvana Home Base" — speaks directly to the accommodation gap they live every visit
- "The Inner Circle" — community-first framing, exclusivity

**What to push for:** a name that references either ownership, the community, or the lifestyle — not the investment. The investment angle is the close, not the hook.

---

### Price anchor

**How to calculate it with the team:**

Total the standalone value of every element in the stack. Rough guide:
- Suite: $189K–$259K (the asset itself)
- Yield guarantee (2 years): $18.9K–$25.9K minimum
- Lifetime membership: annual fee × expected years of use (if annual membership is $3K, 20 years = $60K)
- 28 personal use nights: nightly hotel rate × 28 (if nightly rate is $300, that's $8.4K/year)
- Fully managed rental: equivalent property manager fee (typically 15–20% of gross — ask the team for the number)
- Legal support: Bali lawyer fees for similar work ($3K–$8K)

**The frame to use:** "You're not paying $189K for a suite. You're paying $189K for everything in this stack. The suite is just the vehicle."

Push the team to put a number on every element. If they don't know, that's a homework item before the next session.

---

### The one-sentence offer

**Formula:** "When you [action], you get [dream outcome] without [biggest fear], even if [biggest objection]."

**Draft to have ready (refine with the team in the room):**
"Own a suite inside Nirvana, earn 10% guaranteed while you're away, and always have your community waiting when you arrive. No property management. No complexity from abroad. Even if you've never invested in Bali before."

**What to listen for:** When Susi or Natasha says something in the session that makes the room react, that's the line. Write it down immediately. Their instinctive language beats anything pre-written.

**The test:** read the sentence out loud. If it sounds like a brochure, it's wrong. If it sounds like something a person would actually say, it's right.
