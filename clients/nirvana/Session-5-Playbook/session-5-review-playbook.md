## Part 1 — Real Conversation Review (45 min, facilitation, no fixed script)

**Objective:** use the team's actual conversations from the past 4 weeks to confirm what's working, catch what's breaking, and sharpen the templates and scripts they'll carry forward in the playbook.

This part has no pre-written content because it depends entirely on what Susi and Natasha bring. Run it as a live diagnostic, not a lecture. Below is what to ask and what to listen for, not what to say.

**Structure for each conversation (repeat for all 4, roughly 10-11 min each):**

1. **Set it up.** Ask them to describe the lead in one line first (buyer type, where they were in the funnel, what happened before this conversation).
2. **Walk it, in order.** Have them read or recount what was actually said, step by step, before anyone comments on it. Don't interrupt to correct yet, just get the full sequence on the table.
3. **Ask what they think happened.** Before giving your own read, ask: "What do you think worked there?" or "Where do you think it went off?" Their own diagnosis tells you what they've already internalized versus what's still invisible to them.
4. **Match it to the frameworks already taught.** Where in FRAME (Frame, Read, Admit, Match, Engage) did it succeed or break? Was there a committed next step? Did Give → Tease → Ask show up in the follow-up, if there was one? You're not introducing new language here, you're pointing at where the existing language applies.
5. **Refine on the spot.** If a phrase, opener, or objection response worked better than what's currently in the playbook draft, capture it word for word and swap it in. Real language beats scripted language every time, this is exactly how Session 3's best closing story (the buyer who converted on property-management performance data) got found.

**Facilitator prompts to have ready:**
- "What did the lead actually say, right before that?" (forces precision, not summary)
- "Where in the call did you feel it turn, one way or the other?"
- "If you had that call again tomorrow, what's the one thing you'd say differently?"
- "Was there a next step by the end? A specific date and time, or something looser?"
- "What did you do right before you'd normally have lost them, that time it worked?"

**What to listen for, by person:**
- **Susi:** does she still skip the opening question and go straight to pitching? Is she ending every call, even the ones that didn't close, with a committed next step? Watch her pacing too, Session 4's note was to slow down and let the client talk roughly 70% of the time. Ask directly if she noticed herself doing that.
- **Natasha:** is she still hedging and softening her language, or has that eased since Session 4? Listen for the moment she gets to the ask, does she get there at all, or does the conversation trail off? Her wins here are the strongest evidence to put in front of her before Part 3.

**Refine, don't just review.** If a template in the current draft playbook doesn't match what's actually working on real calls, change it now, live, with both of them watching. That's the point of this block, the playbook gets finished with their real material, not written for them in advance.

**Format:** go around, both answer each question in turn, keep answers short and specific (numbers and examples, not general impressions).

**Round 1 — What's working now that wasn't before?**
- Ask each of them for one specific thing. Push for a real example, not "follow-up is better", ask "give me the last time that showed up on a real call."
- Facilitator note: this is also where you confirm the training actually changed behavior, not just vocabulary. If they can only describe frameworks, not moments, that's a flag for the playbook's plain-language rule (technical name second, plain description first).

## Part 4 — Playbook Handover (15 min)

**Objective:** walk through the finished written playbook together so both of them know exactly how to use it after today, not just that it exists.

**Deliverable referenced here:** the full written sales playbook (separate document, built from Sessions 1-4 plus whatever got refined live in Part 1, rebuilt as one role-neutral guide usable by anyone who sells this product, not sections split by name). Opens with a one-page sales-process map (first contact to close), then six sections: the offer and value stack, client profiles, discovery, objections and answers, how to close, and follow-up.

**How to walk it:**
- **Start with the process map (page 2).** It's the one page they should be able to picture from memory, every other page is depth behind one of its six stages. Point out the two rules that apply everywhere on that page: every call opens with a quick frame (why you're calling, how it'll run) and every call closes with the next step actually set.
- **The rest is depth, not a script to memorize.** Reach for the relevant section when a real situation calls for it, don't try to hold all of it in your head at once. Same document, different way of using it: Susi will likely want the short version, point her toward the one-line proof points, the Big Domino formula, and the closing scripts as her fast pre-call reference. Natasha will likely read the whole thing end to end, that's fine too, her risk was never forgetting the content, it's confidence using it without a script in front of her, Session 4 and today both showed she performs better without leaning on one too hard.
- **Explain what to do when something isn't covered.** Three options, in order:
  1. Use their own judgment first, most situations are a variation on something already in the playbook, not something genuinely new.
  2. Fall back to the guide/hero frame from Session 2: they're the guide, the buyer is the hero, the question is always "what does the hero need to take the next step," not "what's the exact right line." That frame covers situations no script can.
  3. If it's a real gap (pricing, terms, something that needs Toby's sign-off, or a genuinely new objection), flag it to Toby or Matthieu rather than improvising on something that isn't theirs to decide.
- **Confirm both know where the playbook lives** (shared file, printed copy, wherever it's being kept) and that it's a living document, if something in Part 1 got refined today, that update is already in it.
