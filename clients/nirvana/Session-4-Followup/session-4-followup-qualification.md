## Opening — One-Page Recap of Sessions 1-3 (10 min, read together, no practice yet)

Hand over the recap page (see workbook) and read it together out loud. This is a refresher only — no role-play here. Practice starts in the next block.

**Session 1 recap — what we're selling:** three buyer types, one thing each really wants. Pure Investor — money working without me (like the Singapore-based buyer who signed once the income pool math finally clicked). Lifestyle Investor — a life split between home and Bali, the property pays for itself (like the buyer who realized she already spends $8K/year on Bali stays and memberships). Nirvana Member — I own a piece of this (like the member who said "I feel like I'm already home here, I just don't own any of it"). The one-sentence offer: *"Guaranteed minimum 10% ROI without the nightmare of daily management, even if you've never invested in Bali before."* Buyers don't hesitate on price — they hesitate on "is this real?" Proof beats persuasion: 18 of 32 units sold, construction 5 weeks ahead of schedule. And there's already a prepared answer for the big objections — "is the ROI real?" gets 10% guaranteed in writing plus 18/32 already sold; "what about delays?" gets monthly construction videos, top floor already 5 weeks ahead.

**Session 2 recap — ask before you pitch:** first contact is for getting a reply, not a pitch. The 3 discovery questions, word for word: "This year, or more of a 2027 thing for you?" / "Investment, or do you see yourself using it as a base?" / "Looking at other options, or is this your main focus?" Every buyer has a stated reason and a real one — the real one is usually status: who they become by owning this. The Pure Investor is buying proof they make bold, unconventional moves (like the Singapore buyer). The Nirvana Member is buying belonging to something rare (like the member who didn't want to just visit anymore). One question per WhatsApp message, never all three at once. Mention urgency — 14 units left, price increase in July — at every stage, not just at close.

**Session 3 recap — closing the conversation:** one line does the heavy lifting — *"The Sanctuary is the only wellness real estate exit in Bali that generates income while you own it, in a project that will never be replicated — with 14 units left."* Say it in your own words, not read off a page. Tell a story before the numbers — the Singapore investor story is the one that already works. When they hesitate, find out what's really behind it, then match a story, don't argue back with data. **Whatever the objection turns out to be, bring it back to the value stack from Session 1** — the guarantee, the proof points, the offer already built for exactly this. "It's too expensive" isn't really about affordability — reframe it: *"How much do you already spend per year on Bali stays and memberships? At The Sanctuary, that spend becomes ownership."* State urgency once — repeating it turns a fact into pressure. And no matter how the conversation goes, it never ends without a date, a time, and a reason for the next contact.

## Lesson Block 1 — Getting Back to Leads (30 min: teach, then practice)

**Objective:** close the gap Session 2 flagged but never built out — the leads who had a real conversation and then went quiet.

**The number:** 74 leads in HubSpot had a meeting, showed interest, and went quiet. Most weren't uninterested — they were un-followed-up.

**The goal isn't just to get a reply — it's to earn one.** That means giving before you ask, not asking cold. This is the same "give before ask" principle from Session 2: skip straight to the ask, and the reply rate drops. "Just checking in" fails because it has nothing to give.

**The structure: Give → Tease → Ask.**
- **Give** — a real update or fact (construction progress, units sold, a price change)
- **Tease** — let the urgency show through what you just gave: what they'd be missing if they wait
- **Ask** — one clear, specific, low-friction question

Worked example, annotated: *"Hi [name], it's been a bit since we last spoke.* **[Give]** *We're down to 14 units left, with a price increase in July.* **[Tease]** *Are you still weighing this up for this year?* **[Ask]**"

- What not to say: *"Hey, just checking in — any update?"* — this skips straight to the ask with nothing given.

**Example reactivation messages (these are applications of the rule above, not standalone scripts):**

1. **General lead, gone quiet post-meeting:** *"Hi [name], it's been a bit since we last spoke. Wanted to flag that we're down to 14 units left, with a price increase coming in July. Before anything changes, are you still weighing this up for this year?"*
2. **Went quiet after a site visit:** *"Hi [name], hope all's well since you visited us. A few units have sold since then — we're down to 14 remaining. Curious where things landed on your end. Anything I can send over to help you decide?"*
3. **Existing Nirvana member, never approached:** *"Hi [name], did you know there are only 14 suites left inside the club? A few members have already made the move to own rather than just visit. Worth a quick chat about it?"*
4. **Final attempt, pattern interrupt:** *"Hi [name], fair to say The Sanctuary is off your radar for now? Totally fine either way — just don't want to leave you out on the last available units. Let me know."*

**Magic urgency line** — a sharper, more direct version to have on hand: *"Have you given up on making this happen for you?"* Client-centered on purpose: their goal was never Nirvana's success, it's their own.

**When to use it — the cadence:** if a committed next step (from the recap — date/time/reason) gets missed, that's the trigger, not a calendar reminder to "check in." Same day: reason-based message tied to what was promised. +2 days: second attempt, still specific. +5 days: final personal attempt before the lead reverts to the marketing team's automated sequence (already rewritten, out of scope for this session — see `Session-0-Audit/follow-up-sequences-rewrite.md`).

**Coaching:**
- **Susi:** she'll want to move fast to a call rather than write a message — fine, the same rule applies on the phone: lead with something real, then ask.
- **Natasha:** she tends to hedge and soften messages until there's nothing left. Watch for this — the message should read confident, not apologetic.

**Practice (end of this block):** each of them pulls one real cold lead from their own pipeline and drafts the actual reactivation message they'd send, live. Then each identifies one follow-up they currently owe and rewrites it using the value/ask rule instead of "just checking in."

## Lesson Block 2 — The Full Call, Start to Finish (45 min: teach, then practice)

**Objective:** put everything from Sessions 1-3 and today's follow-up rule into one script they can actually follow on a real call, top to bottom.

**Magic sentence, before F:** always open the call by asking permission — *"Is this a bad time for you?"* Getting them to answer gives them the feeling of control, and the act of answering at all is already their first small yes.

**The call, in five plain steps (FRAME), each with a Sanctuary-specific example:**

| Letter | Step | What it means | Sanctuary example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F** | Frame | Set expectations up front — tell them what this call will cover | *"Today I'll walk you through The Sanctuary and answer what's on your mind about the 14 units left."* |
| **R** | Read | Assume, don't ask — make an informed guess about their situation instead of firing off a question list | *"Sounds like this is mainly a long-term investment for you, not somewhere you'd actually stay — fair?"* |
| **A** | Admit | Lay out the problem and get them to say it back in their own words, before you've pitched anything | *"So the real question is whether the 10% guarantee actually holds up — is that what's stopping you?"* |
| **M** | Match | Your solution — the value and story that fits what they just admitted | *"That's why it's guaranteed in writing for 2 years, and 18 of 32 units are already sold."* |
| **E** | Engage | Would they do it, if it adds up? This is the ask | *"If that feels solid, is this something you'd move on this year?"* |

**Two things that always sit around Engage, not new letters:**
- **If Engage doesn't land clean and they hesitate** — that's exactly the moment for the objection tools from Session 3: figure out what's really behind the hesitation, respond with the story that fits it, don't argue with more numbers.
- **However Engage goes, the call doesn't end without a next step** — a date, a time, and a reason. Same rule as Session 3. This is non-negotiable, not optional.

**The practice is a live role-play, not a narration.** By default, Matthieu plays the lead so both salespeople work the framework against a real (if simulated) counterpart, rather than describing what they'd say about someone who isn't in the room.

**The lead Matthieu plays — "David":** met at the Nirvana gym three weeks ago. Timeline: deciding this year. Pure Investor — cares about capital efficiency, wants zero hands-on management. What he's said before: *"The 10% guarantee sounds good, but I've heard promises like that before."* This gives Susi and Natasha a real Admit-stage hesitation to work with (matches the guarantee doubt already built into the Call Framework's A-row example), and Matthieu can play him warmer or more skeptical depending on how each of them is doing.

**If they'd rather use a real lead instead**, it needs to meet three conditions: (1) someone they've already spoken with at least once, not a cold contact; (2) they can say, off the top of their head, the lead's rough timeline, what they care about most, and any hesitation already heard; (3) if no lead checks all three boxes, the closest match is fine — say out loud where the gaps are, that's useful information too.

**Practice (end of this block):** one full call, start to finish, following FRAME plus the two rules above, played out live against Matthieu-as-David (or the substitute real lead). Susi goes first to model it; Natasha follows. If Natasha freezes anywhere, stop, sit with it a moment, and let her try again rather than supplying the answer — where she gets stuck is exactly the information Session 5 needs.

## Lesson Block 3 — Tower 1 Positioning (30 min: teach, then practice)

**Objective:** stop selling Tower 1 as "Tower 2 minus the sauna." Give it its own pitch, matched to the buyer type from Read/Match above.

**The core problem:** Tower 1 ground floor units get framed as the discount option. No buyer wants to consciously choose the lesser version at nearly the same price. Fix: Tower 1 is a different product for a different buyer, not a worse version of Tower 2.

**Two buyer types:**

**Pure Investor** — doesn't care about private amenities, cares about capital efficiency.

> "You're not paying $80K extra for a sauna you'd use 28 days a year. You're investing $179K for the exact same ROI percentage as a $259K unit — same income pool, same guarantee, same management. That's the most capital-efficient entry into this development."

**Lifestyle Investor (Nirvana regular)** — already part of the community, wants to be physically embedded in it.

> "Ground level means you wake up inside the facility. No elevator, direct terrace access, 30 steps from the gym, the pool, the restaurant. Tower 2 has the view. Tower 1 has the life."

**What Tower 1 buyers get (pending Toby's sign-off on 1 and 3):**
1. 3-year ROI guarantee instead of 2 (Tower 2's term)
2. Priority personal-use booking window within the 28 personal days
3. Founding Investor recognition — last units in the development, ever
4. Monthly construction update (photo/video from the site manager) — costs nothing, builds trust during the build

**Urgency, reframed:** not "still available" — "the last remaining inventory in this development, ever. Tower 2 is gone. Once these 14 are gone, there's no future phase at any price."

**What to avoid:** never compare Tower 1 and Tower 2 in the same sentence. Don't lead with square meters or floor level — lead with the buyer type. Don't discount. Don't apologize for the product — the missing sauna is "you're not paying for amenities you don't need," not a gap.

**Practice (end of this block):** given a lead's answer from Read/Match, identify which buyer type it signals, then pitch the matching Tower 1 version, ending on the urgency line.
