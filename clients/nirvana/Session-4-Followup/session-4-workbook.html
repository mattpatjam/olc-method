What Mattered From Sessions 1-3
10 minutes · read together, no exercise on this page

This is a refresher, not a test. Practice starts in the next section, on things we're about to cover today — nothing here gets rehearsed cold.

Session 1 — What We're Selling

Three buyer types, one thing each really wants: Pure Investor — money working without me (like the Singapore buyer who signed once the income pool math clicked). Lifestyle Investor — a life split between home and Bali, the property pays for itself (like the buyer who realized she already spends $8K/year on Bali stays). Nirvana Member — I own a piece of this (like the member who said "I'm already home here, I just don't own any of it").

The one-sentence offer: "Guaranteed minimum 10% ROI without the nightmare of daily management, even if you've never invested in Bali before."

They don't hesitate on price — they hesitate on "is this real?" Proof beats persuasion: 18 of 32 units sold, construction 5 weeks ahead of schedule.

There's already a prepared answer for the big objections — "is the ROI real?" gets 10% guaranteed in writing plus 18/32 already sold; "what about delays?" gets monthly construction videos, top floor already 5 weeks ahead.

Session 2 — Ask Before You Pitch

First contact is for getting a reply, not a pitch. The 3 discovery questions, word for word: "This year, or more of a 2027 thing for you?" / "Investment, or do you see yourself using it as a base?" / "Looking at other options, or is this your main focus?"

Every buyer has a stated reason and a real one — the real one is usually status: who they become by owning this. The Pure Investor is buying proof they make bold, unconventional moves (like the Singapore buyer). The Nirvana Member is buying belonging to something rare (like the member who didn't want to just visit anymore).

One question per WhatsApp message, never all three at once. Mention urgency — 14 units left, price increase in July — at every stage, not just at close.

Session 3 — Closing the Conversation

One line does the heavy lifting:

"The Sanctuary is the only wellness real estate exit in Bali that generates income while you own it, in a project that will never be replicated — with 14 units left."

Say it in your own words. Tell a story before the numbers — the Singapore investor story is the one that already works. When they hesitate, find out what's really behind it, then match a story, don't argue with data.

Whatever the objection turns out to be, bring it back to the value stack from Session 1 — the guarantee, the proof points, the offer already built for exactly this. "It's too expensive" isn't really about affordability — reframe it: "How much do you already spend per year on Bali stays and memberships? At The Sanctuary, that spend becomes ownership."

State urgency once — repeating it turns a fact into pressure. And it never ends without a date, a time, and a reason.

Lesson 1 · Getting Back to Leads

Follow-Up Message
30 minutes · teach, then practice below

74 leads in HubSpot had a meeting, showed interest, and went quiet. Most weren't uninterested — they were un-followed-up.

The Goal

The goal isn't just to get a reply — it's to earn one. That means giving before you ask, the same "give before ask" rule from Session 2. "Just checking in" fails because it skips straight to asking with nothing given first.

The Structure: Give → Tease → Ask
Give

A real update or fact — construction progress, units sold, a price change

Tease

Let urgency show through what you gave — what they'd miss if they wait

Ask

One clear, specific, low-friction question

Example Messages
General lead, post-meeting — annotated

"Hi [name], it's been a bit since we last spoke. [GIVE] We're down to 14 units left, with a price increase in July. [TEASE] Are you still weighing this up for this year? [ASK]"

Went quiet after a site visit

"Hi [name], hope all's well since your visit. A few units have sold since — we're down to 14. Anything I can send to help you decide?"

Existing member, never approached

"Hi [name], did you know there are only 14 suites left inside the club? A few members have already made the move to own. Worth a quick chat?"

Magic urgency line

"Have you given up on making this happen for you?"

When to Use It — The Cadence
WhenWhat
Same dayReason-based message tied to the missed commitment, not a generic check-in
+2 daysSecond attempt, still specific, still one clear ask
+5 daysFinal personal attempt before it reverts to the marketing team's automated sequence

Lesson 2 · The Full Call
2
Call Framework
45 minutes · teach, then practice on the next page

Everything from Sessions 1-3 and today's follow-up rule, put into one call you can actually follow, top to bottom.

Magic sentence, before F: always open by asking permission — "Is this a bad time for you?" Getting them to answer gives them the feeling of control, and answering at all is already their first small yes.

StepWhat it meansSanctuary example
F
Frame		 Set expectations up front "Today I'll walk you through The Sanctuary and answer what's on your mind about the 14 units left."
R
Read		 Assume, don't ask — guess their situation instead of a question list "Sounds like this is mainly a long-term investment for you, not somewhere you'd actually stay — fair?"
A
Admit		 Get them to say the real problem, before you pitch "So the real question is whether the 10% guarantee actually holds up — is that what's stopping you?"
M
Match		 Your solution — the value and story that fits "That's why it's guaranteed in writing for 2 years, and 18 of 32 units are already sold."
E
Engage		 Would they do it, if it adds up? This is the ask "If that feels solid, is this something you'd move on this year?"

Two things that always sit around Engage: if they hesitate here, that's the moment for Session 3's objection tools — figure out what's really behind it, respond with the story that fits. And however Engage goes, the call doesn't end without a next step: a date, a time, a reason. Non-negotiable.

The Practice: Role-Play, or a Real Lead

By default, Matthieu plays the example lead on the next page — a live role-play, not a narration. If you'd rather use a real lead of your own instead, it needs to meet these three conditions:

1
Someone you've already spoken with at least once — not a cold, unknown contact.
2
You can say, off the top of your head: their timeline, what they care about most, and anything that sounded like hesitation.
3
No perfect match? Pick the closest lead and say out loud where the gaps are — that's useful information too.

Lesson 3 · Tower 1 Positioning
3
Not "Tower 2 Minus the Sauna"
30 minutes · teach, then practice on the next page

Tower 1 ground floor keeps getting sold as the discount version of Tower 2. Fix: a different offer for a different buyer — not a worse version of Tower 2.

Pure Investor

Doesn't care about private amenities. Cares about capital efficiency.

"$179K for the exact same ROI percentage as a $259K unit — same pool, same guarantee, same management."

Lifestyle Investor

Already part of the community. Wants to be physically embedded in it.

"Ground level means you wake up inside the facility. Tower 2 has the view. Tower 1 has the life."

What Tower 1 Buyers Get
1
3-year ROI guarantee instead of 2 (pending Toby's sign-off).
2
Priority booking — first pick on the 28 personal-use days.
3
Founding Investor recognition (pending Toby's sign-off).
4
Monthly construction update — costs nothing, builds trust.
Urgency, Reframed

Not "still available" — the last remaining inventory in this development, ever. Tower 2 is gone. Once these 14 are gone, there's no future phase at any price.

What to Avoid

Never compare Tower 1 and Tower 2 in the same sentence. Don't lead with square meters or floor level — lead with the buyer type. Don't discount. Don't apologize — the missing sauna is "you're not paying for amenities you don't need," not a gap.
