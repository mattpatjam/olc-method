# Nirvana Health Club — Post-Training Pitch

*How to propose the new model to Toby after the training program ends.*

---

## Context

Current engagement: flat-fee sales training ($2,650 total, 7-week program starting next week).
Objective: after training ends, pitch Toby on a performance-based sales engagement where Matthieu runs the actual sales process for a unit campaign.

Training ends: approximately Week 8 from now.
Pitch window: final training session or a dedicated follow-up meeting.

---

## The Transition Framing

Don't pitch this as a new product. Frame it as the natural next step:

> "The training was about giving your team the foundation. What we're talking about now is putting that foundation to work on a real campaign — where I come in alongside you, close the first wave of units, and prove that everything we built together actually converts."

The training built trust and demonstrated competence. The pitch is just asking to use that trust on a performance-only basis.

---

## What to Propose

**Phase 1: Discovery + Offer Design**
- 2-hour session with Toby (and head of sales if there is one)
- Output: the Grand Slam Offer for Nirvana apartments — what Matthieu would sell, how it's framed, what value is stacked for buyers
- Output: finalized campaign strategy brief — which channels, budget guidance, buyer profile
- Fee: scoped onboarding fee (TBD based on benchmark research)

**Phase 2: Campaign Launch**
- Toby runs a targeted ad campaign (client pays ad spend directly)
- Matthieu handles inbound lead qualification, discovery calls, property demos, and closings
- Duration: 60–90 days for the first campaign cycle
- Fee: performance % per unit closed (TBD)

**Guarantee to offer:**
> "If we don't close any units in 60 days from campaign launch, I refund the strategy fee in full. You lose nothing."

---

## Questions to Ask Toby Before Pitching

1. How many units do you currently have available for sale?
2. What's the target price per unit?
3. What have you been spending on marketing / ads per month?
4. What's your current close rate — how many qualified leads does it take to close one unit?
5. Who is your typical buyer? (nationality, budget range, motivations)
6. Do you have any existing agency relationships or exclusive contracts I should know about?

These answers shape the offer and the performance fee math. Don't pitch until you have the answers.

---

## Objection Handling

**"We already have agents working on it."**
> "I'm not replacing your agents — I'm supplementing them. They handle the relationships they already have. I run a structured campaign to reach buyers who've never heard of Nirvana before. More volume, not competition."

**"Why would I pay you more than a standard agent?"**
> "A standard agent brings you leads. I design the offer that makes people want to buy before they even talk to someone, then I close them. You're paying for the system and the results, not just the introduction."

**"What if it doesn't work?"**
> "That's why there's a guarantee. If no units close in 60 days from launch, I refund the strategy fee. You're risking the ad spend — which you'd be paying anyway if you ran ads — not my fee."

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Completely fair. What would you need to see to make this a yes? Is it the pricing, the timeline, or something about the approach that doesn't feel right?" (Voss: use calibrated questions, not pressure)

---

## What Success Looks Like

- Toby says yes to the discovery + offer design session
- After the session, he sees a substantially better framing of what he's selling
- He commits to a 60-day campaign and ad budget
- Matthieu closes at least 1–2 units in that period
- This becomes the first case study for the OLC Method
