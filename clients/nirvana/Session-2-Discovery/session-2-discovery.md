## The Five Rules (10 min — open with these)

Before any tactics. Read each one out loud. After each rule, ask: "Where did you see this last week?"

**Rule 5:** People buy with emotion and they justify it with logic.

**Rule 4:** People will spend more to avoid pain than to gain pleasure.

**Rule 3:** Sales is not about telling the customer what you need. It is about asking the right questions that get them to want.

**Rule 2:** The prospect must feel the consequences of inaction. Every sale, ask: what will happen if nothing changes today?

**Rule 1:** Stop caring so much about whether they buy. Be willing to walk away. The harder you try, the more doubt you imply.

**Facilitation note:** Don't lecture these. One rule, one pause, one real example from last week. If neither can give an example, give yours. Keep it under 10 minutes — this is setup, not content. Rules 3 and 4 connect directly to the discovery framework. Rule 2 drives how the reactivation templates are written. Rule 1 is the coaching note Susi needs before any role play.

## Part 1 — Why First Contact Kills Deals (15 min)

**Open with this number:** 74.

74 leads in HubSpot had a conversation, showed interest, and went quiet. They were not uninterested — they were un-followed-up.

Ask the team: "What does the first message to a lead who's gone quiet usually look like?"

Common answer: "Just checking in." "Any update?" "Still interested?"

These messages do not work. They put the burden on the buyer. They also signal low confidence — a salesperson who needs the buyer to rescue the conversation.

**The reframe (Rule 2):** A re-engagement message should make the buyer feel something. Either the possibility they are missing, or the cost of waiting. Not pressure — consequence.

**The energy layer:** A message is only half of it. When Susi picks up the phone, the buyer feels her energy before she says a word. When Natasha sends a message, the tone either opens or closes the conversation before the content does. Scripts without energy are just words.

**Show genuine interest in the person, not the deal.** The buyers coming to Nirvana are not random leads. They are people considering something meaningful: a first investment abroad, a change in how they live, a connection to a community they already care about. Ask real questions and actually listen to the answers. They will feel the difference between someone collecting data and someone genuinely curious about where they are in life. That interest is itself a differentiator. Most salespeople they have spoken to were collecting information. You are trying to understand them.

**This applies differently to each profile:**
- Susi (DI): High energy naturally, but it fires before she has really listened. The buyer feels the pitch coming before it arrives. Slowing down the first two minutes and genuinely receiving what the buyer says is what makes the energy land.
- Natasha (SC): Naturally warm and attentive, but the warmth stays internal. The buyer doesn't feel it coming back through the message. The coaching point: project it outward. Short, direct, confident. Trust what you know.

## Part 2 — The Discovery Framework (30 min)

Three questions. Not a form. A listening map — three angles that tell you everything you need to know before you say anything about the product.

**The guide frame:**
Susi and Natasha are not sellers. They are guides. The buyer is the hero — trying to reach something real: financial security, a Bali base, proof to themselves that they make bold moves. A guide's job is not to convince the hero of anything. It is to understand where the hero is, show them the path, and walk alongside them.

Discovery is the guide's first act. You cannot guide someone you do not understand. Every question below is not extraction — it is a guide learning what kind of hero they are dealing with and where this particular journey starts.

The buyer will feel the difference between a salesperson collecting data and a guide genuinely trying to understand where they are in life. That difference is why Rule 3 exists. Stop caring about the sale. Start caring about the buyer.

**What they are really buying:**
Every buyer has a stated reason and a real one. The stated reason is investment return or a Bali base. The real reason is status: who they become when they own this. The investor is buying proof that they make bold, unconventional moves. The lifestyle buyer is buying belonging to something rare. (This connects directly to Expert Secrets — Russell Brunson's framework on identity: people don't buy products, they buy the version of themselves the product represents.) Your job in discovery is not just to categorize the buyer. It is to understand what identity they are reaching for. Show them this is exactly how they get there, and the pitch sells itself.

**Facilitation note:** Ask the team: "When was the last time YOU bought something where the real reason was not what you told yourself?" This gets them to the concept without lecturing. It usually produces a good laugh and a real example.

**Question 1 — Timeline**
"Are you looking at something for this year, or is this more of a 2027 situation for you?"

What it tells you: urgency and decision readiness. "This year" means close. "2027" means qualify differently or move on.

**Question 2 — Profile**
"Are you thinking of this primarily as an investment, or do you actually see yourself using it as a base in Bali?"

What it tells you: which dream outcome they want. Pure Investor vs. True Believer. The pitch after this question is completely different.

**Question 3 — Comparison**
"Are you looking at other options right now, or is The Sanctuary your main focus?"

What it tells you: how to position and how much urgency to use. Comparing = differentiate. Main focus = remove the final obstacle.

---

**Two delivery versions:**

**Message version (Natasha — HubSpot/WhatsApp):**
One question per touchpoint. The goal of the first message is a reply. Once they reply, the next message goes one level deeper. Never send all three at once.

Structure: warm opener → one question → easy off-ramp.

Example: "Hi [name], picking this up from where we left it. Are you thinking about a move like this for this year, or is it more of a 2027 plan for you? Either way, happy to work around your timeline."

**Phone version (Susi — first call or reactivation call):**
All three in the first two minutes, naturally. Not a checklist — a conversation. The transition between questions sounds like genuine curiosity, not an intake form.

Example opening: "Good to reconnect. Before I go into anything, let me understand where you are — are you looking at this for this year? And are you thinking of it as a pure investment, or do you actually see yourself coming to Bali and staying here? Great. Are you looking at anything else alongside The Sanctuary right now?"
Then pitch to their profile.

**Facilitation note for the group:**
Susi will want to move to the pitch immediately — she knows the product cold. The coaching point: "You already know how to pitch. Discovery is not about learning the product. It is about wasting zero time on the wrong pitch. The right discovery cuts your call in half." Frame it as efficiency, not restraint.

Natasha already asks careful questions but buries them in long messages. The coaching point: short message, one question, stop. Let the reply do the work.

**Round 2 — Rule 1 in practice (5 min)**

Ask Susi: "You have been following up with a lead for three weeks. They keep saying 'not yet.' What do you say next?"

Common answer: another follow-up, slightly different wording.

The right answer (Rule 1): "Look, I don't want to keep chasing if the timing isn't right. If you're ready to look at this properly this year, let's find a time. If not, I'll check back in three months — no pressure either way."

This is the hardest thing to do and the most effective. The buyer feels the detachment. They respect it. They often call back.
