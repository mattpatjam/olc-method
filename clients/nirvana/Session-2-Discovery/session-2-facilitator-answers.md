## Page 3 — The One-Sentence Offer

**What a strong final version sounds like:**

The formula: "When you [action], you get [dream outcome] without [biggest fear], even if [biggest objection]."

The draft in the workbook is close but reads like a brochure. The test is whether Susi could say it naturally on a call to an existing member and have the person lean in.

**Target version to guide toward:**

"Own a suite inside Nirvana, earn 10% guaranteed while you're away, and always have your people waiting when you arrive. Fully managed, zero complexity from abroad. Even if you've never invested in Bali."

**What makes it better than the draft:**
- "your people" instead of "your community" — more personal, less marketing
- Remove the em dash and the formal structure
- It should feel like a sentence said out loud, not written for a deck

**What makes a bad version:**
- Any version that starts with "invest in" or "purchase" — too transactional
- Any version that leads with yield before belonging — wrong order for this buyer profile
- Anything over two sentences

**What to listen for in the room:**
When Susi says something during the exercise that makes Natasha nod, that's the line. Write it down immediately. Their instinctive language beats anything pre-written.

**If they want to rewrite it completely:**
Let them. The goal is ownership, not the exact wording. A sentence they invented and believe is worth more than a perfect sentence they memorized.

## Page 4 — Discovery Framework

### What each question is really listening for

**Question 1: Timeline**
"Are you looking at something for this year, or is this more of a 2027 situation for you?"

*What different buyers say and how to follow the thread:*

- "This year" — strong signal. Qualify on profile next, then move fast. Use urgency in the same conversation: 14 units, August target, July price increase.
- "2027 or later" — don't drop them. Qualify on profile, send one piece of useful content (not a brochure), and set a recheck date. Don't spend the same energy as a 2025 buyer.
- "I'm not sure" — means they haven't made the decision to look. This is a pre-qualified lead. Warm them first. Don't pitch.

**Question 2: Profile**
"Are you thinking of this primarily as an investment, or do you actually see yourself using it as a base in Bali?"

*What different buyers say and how to follow the thread:*

- "Pure investment" — lead with yield. $179K. Same pool return as Tower 2 (confirm with Toby). Management fee fully covered. 10% contractual minimum. No management from abroad.
- "Both, investment but I'd come too" — the Lifestyle Investor. Lead with belonging: "Imagine arriving at the airport and knowing your suite is ready, your gym membership is waiting, and you're already home." Yield is the rational justification, not the hook.
- "I'm already a member, this is about having my own place here" — this is Profile C, the True Believer. The highest-conversion profile. They are already sold on Nirvana. You are just closing the accommodation gap. Lead with: "You already come here. What if the suite was yours?"

*The status layer (keep in your head, don't pitch it directly):*
Every investor is also buying proof that they make unconventional, bold moves. Every lifestyle buyer is buying belonging to something rare. When the pitch lands and they feel it, that's because you touched the identity, not the feature. You're not explaining the product. You're reflecting back who they get to be.

**Question 3: Comparison**
"Are you looking at other options right now, or is The Sanctuary your main focus?"

*What different buyers say and how to follow the thread:*

- "Just The Sanctuary" — they're ready. Your job is to remove the final obstacle. Find out what it is. The follow-up question ("what's the main thing you're still unsure about right now?") is mandatory here.
- "Looking at a few things" — ask what else. If they're comparing another Bali condo: differentiate on management, yield structure, and club membership. If they're comparing a villa: "You're not comparing the same product. A villa gives you space and a pool. This gives you a performing asset, a management team, a community, and a lifetime gym membership. Different thing entirely."
- "I haven't looked at anything else yet" — this is early. They need information before they need a close. Give them the key facts, ask the follow-up question, set a clear next step.

**Follow-up question:**
"What's the main thing you're still unsure about right now?"

*Common answers and how to handle them:*

- "The construction timeline" — construction is ahead of schedule. Get the specific milestone from Toby before the session and have it ready.
- "Whether the 10% is real" — contractual minimum, written into the management agreement, first 2 years. Then performance-based. This is not projected, it's guaranteed. Be direct.
- "The leasehold structure / what happens at year 27" — waiting for Toby's answer. If not received by June 25: "I'm getting the formal answer from our legal team this week and I'll send it to you before the end of the day." Do not guess.
- "I need my financial advisor to review it" — "Of course. I can send the legal and financial documentation directly to your advisor. What's the best email?" Move the process forward. Don't treat this as a no.
- "I need to think about it" — this is a soft objection. Use Rule 1: "Absolutely. Take the time you need. Just so you know, there are 14 units left and we have an August close-out target. If you want to be sure your preferred unit is still available when you're ready, we can reserve it for $10K, fully refundable within 14 days. No commitment to the full purchase." This turns "think about it" into a low-risk reservation.

## Page 5 — Template Workshop

### Template 1: Reactivation (the 74 warm leads)

**What a strong version looks like:**

The draft in the workbook is good. The team should personalize it. Watch for two failure modes:

- **Too soft (Natasha's risk):** "Hi [name], hope you're well. I just wanted to follow up in case you had any questions about The Sanctuary. Let me know if you'd like any more information." This does nothing. No reason to reply.
- **Too pushy (Susi's risk on a message):** "Hi [name], we only have 14 units left and prices are going up in July. Let me know if you want to book a call." Technically correct but no warmth. The buyer feels sold at.

**Target version (stronger than the draft):**

"Hi [name], checking in because a few things have moved since we last spoke. 14 units left and a price adjustment is coming in July. I want to make sure you have the full picture before anything changes. Are you still thinking about this for this year?"

Why it works:
- "A few things have moved" is curiosity-opening, not pressure
- The urgency (units, price) is fact, not threat
- Ends with the timeline question — Rule 3 in action

**Susi's phone version of this:**
"[Name], it's Susi from The Sanctuary. I'm calling because things are moving and I don't want you to miss the window. We're down to 14 units and a price increase is coming in July. I just want to make sure you have everything you need to make a decision. Are you still thinking about this for this year, or has the timing changed?"

### Template 2: New Lead (first contact)

**What a strong version looks like:**

This is Natasha's primary tool. The draft asks the profile question (investment vs. base), which is correct. Push the team to make it sound more human.

**Target version (stronger than the draft):**

"Hi [name], thanks for reaching out. Before I send anything your way, one quick question: are you thinking of this as an investment, or are you actually planning to come to Bali and use it as a base? It changes what I share with you, so I want to make sure what I send is actually relevant."

Why it works:
- "Before I send anything your way" signals respect for their time
- "It changes what I share" is honest and useful — buyer feels they're getting something tailored
- The last line removes the pressure of a sales pitch

**What to watch in Natasha's version:**
She will tend to over-explain and soften the ask. The question should land in two sentences max. If her draft runs past three sentences, have her cut it.

### Template 3: Nirvana Member Approach

**What a strong version looks like:**

This is the highest-opportunity template in the session. 700 people per day through the club. Zero systematic outreach. The in-person version is:

"Where are you staying while you're here?"
[They name somewhere else.]
"Did you know you can own a suite inside the club? Fully managed, lifetime membership from day one, pays you 10% a year while you're away. Worth a quick conversation?"

**What makes this template different:**
It doesn't open with the product. It opens with a question about them. The product emerges naturally from the answer. This is Rule 3 and the give-give-give principle at the same time.

**Push the team on the WhatsApp version for members who aren't on site:**

"Hi [name], it's Susi from Nirvana. Quick question: when you're in Bali, where do you usually stay? I ask because there's something you might not know about yet. Happy to share if it's relevant."

This is softer but works for someone the team has a relationship with. It leads with curiosity, not pitch.

## Role Play — What to Watch For

**Natasha (Round 1 — sends Template 1 live):**
Watch the language she uses when she writes in real time. The live version will reveal whether she has internalized the principle or is just copying the draft. If she hedges ("I just wanted to..." "I hope you're doing well..."), stop immediately and ask: "If you received this message, would you reply?" She knows the answer. That's the coaching moment.

The goal is confidence in the message. Short. Direct. One question. No apology for reaching out.

**Susi (Round 1 — phone version of reactivation):**
Watch whether she goes straight to discovery or straight to pitch. The exercise is specifically to open with the timeline question before any product mention. If she pitches first, pause and ask: "What did you learn about this buyer in that first 30 seconds?" She'll realize she learned nothing. That's the insight.

**Round 2 — Rule 1 in practice:**
The right answer to "they keep saying not yet" is not another follow-up with slightly different wording. The right answer is detachment with a clear offer:

"Look, I don't want to keep following up if the timing's not right. If you're ready to look at this properly this year, let's set a time. If not, I'll check back in a few months. No pressure either way."

Susi will resist this instinctively — it feels like giving up. Frame it as confidence. The buyers who respect this call back. The ones who don't weren't going to buy anyway.

## Urgency Language — Exact Phrases

Have these ready. Use them naturally, not as a script.

*At first contact:*
"Just so you have the full picture: we're down to 14 units and there's a price adjustment coming in July."

*In a reactivation call:*
"I'm reaching out now specifically because things are moving faster than expected. I want to make sure you have a chance to act before the options narrow."

*When they say "I'll think about it":*
"Of course. Just know that the unit we were looking at is still available, but I can't guarantee for how long. If you want to secure it while you're deciding, the reservation is $10K and fully refundable within 14 days."

*Never:*
"There's a lot of interest in this property." (Vague. Feels fake.)
"I'd hate for you to miss out." (Needy. Susi especially should avoid this.)
