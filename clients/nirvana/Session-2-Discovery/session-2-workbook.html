The Five Rules
·
Before Tactics, There Are Rules
The foundation every sales technique is built on

These are not frameworks. They are facts about how people decide. Everything in this session (the discovery questions, the templates, the role plays) is an application of one or more of these rules.

5
People buy with emotion and justify it with logic.
Features close the mind. Feelings close the deal. Lead with the outcome, then back it with proof.
At The Sanctuary: Lead with arriving to find the suite ready and the gym pass active. Then back it with the numbers: 10% guaranteed, 14 units, July increase.
4
People will spend more to avoid pain than to gain pleasure.
The cost of waiting (missing units, a price increase, losing the best option) is more powerful than the promise of a return.
At The Sanctuary: "14 units. July price increase. August sell-out target." These land harder than any projected return.
3
Sales is not about telling the customer what they need. It is about asking the right questions that make them want.
You don't convince anyone. You ask the right question and let them get there themselves.
At The Sanctuary: Don't pitch the yield. Ask their timeline. Their answer tells you which close to use, and which objection to address first.
2
The prospect must feel the consequences of inaction. Every sale, ask: what will happen if nothing changes today?
That answer is your close. Not the features, not the price. The cost of not deciding.
At The Sanctuary: When they say "I'll think about it," ask: "What happens if nothing changes today?" That answer is your close.
1
Stop caring so much about whether they buy. Be willing to walk away. The harder you try, the more doubt you imply.
Detachment is not indifference. It is confidence. The buyer feels it before you say a word.
At The Sanctuary: "I don't want to keep following up if the timing's not right. If you're ready, let's set a time. If not, I'll check back in a few months." Then stop.

Rule 3 is why we ask discovery questions. Rule 2 is why the reactivation templates work. Rule 1 is what makes the difference between a reply and silence.

Why First Contact Kills Deals
15 minutes

74. That's how many leads in HubSpot had a conversation, showed interest, and went quiet. They were not uninterested. They just never got a follow-up.

What doesn't work, and why

"Just checking in." "Any update?" "Still interested?" These put the burden on the buyer. They also signal low confidence: a salesperson who needs the buyer to rescue the conversation.

The reframe (Rule 2)

A re-engagement message should make the buyer feel something: the possibility they are missing, or the cost of waiting. Not pressure; consequence. That consequence is your close.

The energy layer

When Susi picks up the phone, the buyer feels her energy before she says a word. When Natasha sends a message, the tone opens or closes the conversation before the content does. Show genuine interest in the person, not the deal. The buyer feels the difference between someone collecting data and someone genuinely curious about where they are in life.

Susi

The Discovery Framework
1
Three Questions Before Any Pitch
30 minutes · Framework + delivery

The people you are talking to are not just buying a property. They are stepping into a new experience, and for most of them it is their first time investing in Bali. Your job is to guide them through it. That starts before any product conversation does.

What they are really buying

Every buyer has a stated reason and a real one. Beneath the investment return or the Bali base is a status driver: who they become when they own this. The investor is buying proof they make bold, unconventional moves. The lifestyle buyer is buying belonging to something rare. Discovery is how you find out which identity they are reaching for. Show them this is exactly how they get there, and the pitch sells itself.

Give, give, give, then ask. Every time you reach out, lead with something useful: a real update, a clear reason for the message, a fact they didn't have. Ask your question after. If you ask before giving anything, the chance of a reply drops sharply. The templates later in this workbook are built on this principle.

The goal of first contact is a reply. Not a pitch, not a close. A reply. Everything else comes after.

1
Timeline
"Are you looking at something for this year, or is this more of a 2027 situation for you?"
Tells you urgency and decision readiness. "This year" means ready to move. "2027" means qualify differently and don't put the same time in.
2
Profile
"Are you thinking of this primarily as an investment, or do you actually see yourself using it as a base in Bali?"
Tells you which outcome they want. Pure Investor means pitch the yield math. True Believer means pitch community, belonging, and a home base. The pitch is completely different depending on the answer.

The Discovery Questions
3
Comparison
"Are you looking at other options right now, or is The Sanctuary your main focus?"
Tells you how to position. Comparing means differentiate first. Main focus means remove the final obstacle. If they've already decided, don't add pressure. Help them commit.
+
Follow-up
"What's the main thing you're still unsure about right now?"
Ask this once the three questions are answered. An objection raised here is a question you can answer. The same objection raised at the close is a wall. Surface it in discovery, frame it now, and you walk into the close with nothing left to fight.
In practice
Message · Natasha / HubSpot
One question per message. Never send all three at once. Get a reply first, then go one level deeper.
"Hi [name], picking this up from where we left it. Are you thinking about a move like this for this year, or is it more of a 2027 plan? Either way, happy to work around your timeline."
Phone · Susi
All three in the first two minutes, naturally, not as a checklist. Genuine curiosity, not a form. Then pitch to the profile they gave you.
"Before I go into anything, are you looking at this for this year? Investment or a base in Bali? And are you comparing other options or is this your main focus?"

Why discovery closes faster: every question here is also an objection check. You're not just learning who they are; you're finding out what's in the way. Address it in discovery and it disappears from the close. Skip it and it comes back as "I need to think about it."

Active Listening
2
The ACA Framework: How to Respond
Use this after every discovery answer

The three questions tell you who the buyer is. How you respond to their answers is what makes them feel understood, and open. Most salespeople move straight to their next point. ACA makes the buyer feel heard first. That is what closes the gap between information and trust.

A
Acknowledge: affirm what they said
Don't agree or disagree yet. Just signal you received it. This lowers their guard before anything else.
"That makes sense."  /  "Good to know."  /  "I hear you."
C
Clarify & Complement: restate, then tie in a character trait
Reflect back what they said in your own words. Then connect their answer to a positive identity. They feel understood and respected, not sold to. This also surfaces hidden objections: if you restate it wrong, they'll correct you and reveal what's really on their mind.
"So what I'm hearing is: you want the return, but you'd actually come and enjoy it too. That's a smart way to look at it. The buyers I respect most think the same way: they want the asset working for them, and something they're proud to own."
A
Ask: lead the conversation where you need it to go
Now that they feel heard, they are open. Ask the question that moves them toward the close, or that surfaces the next objection to address.
"Given that, what would need to be true for this to be a clear yes for you this year?"

ACA is not a script. It is a reflex. Practice it until Acknowledge → Clarify & Complement → Ask happens without thinking. The goal is not to sound like you're using a technique. The goal is to sound like someone who genuinely cares about the answer, and moves the conversation forward because of it.

Note: the C step is where most objections reveal themselves. When you restate and the buyer corrects you, listen hard. What they correct is usually the real concern. Address it there, not at the close.
