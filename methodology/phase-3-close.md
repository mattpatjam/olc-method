## Face-to-Face vs. One-to-Many Closing

These are two fundamentally different skills. The frameworks are related but the execution is opposite.

**One-to-many (webinar, group presentation, pitch event):** You cannot ask follow-up questions. Every false belief, every objection, every buying resistance must be pre-loaded into the presentation script. You identify the likely beliefs in advance and knock them down before they surface. Mini-closes and scripted transitions belong here.

**Face-to-face (1:1 call, WhatsApp conversation, property showing):** The buyer tells you exactly which false belief is active — if you're listening. You don't pre-empt objections; you diagnose them live. The skill is pattern recognition: hear the hesitation, identify the belief category (new opportunity / vehicle / internal / external), deploy the story that breaks that specific belief. Real-time, adaptive, story-driven.

OLC's closing process is face-to-face. The steps below are built for that context.

## Closing Process

### Step 1 — Discovery Call
- Open with calibrated questions: understand where the buyer is, what's driving them, and what they've tried before
- Never pitch before the motivation is clear
- Identify: wealth / health / relationships — which core desire is this purchase serving?
- Qualify against the ICP green light criteria from Phase 1C

### Step 2 — Buyer Epiphany Story
- Before presenting anything, deliver a 60-second story about a buyer in the same position who made the decision
- Match the story to the buyer's profile identified in Step 1 (wealth / health / relationships driver)
- Speak at a plain, simple level — no projections, no percentages, no jargon
- The goal: the buyer feels the decision before they see the logic. The value stack lands differently once they're in that emotional state
- Rule: story before logic, always. Skipping this step and leading with the value stack is asking the buyer to make a logical decision under pressure — most people can't and won't

**How to build a buyer epiphany story (5-step short Epiphany Bridge):**

1. **Back story** — who was this buyer, where were they, what were they trying to achieve
2. **The journey** — what made them start looking (the trigger), what was holding them back (the villain: a false belief, a bad past experience, a missed opportunity)
3. **New opportunity** — the moment everything shifted; what they discovered or who guided them
4. **Framework** — the simple decision or action they took
5. **Achievement** — where they are now, stated in plain human terms (not ROI percentages)

Build one story per buyer profile. Store completed stories in the client playbook. Use the first closed deal for each profile to source the real details — that's what makes it land.

Reference: `expert-secrets-reference.md` section 7 — Epiphany Bridge Script and Hero's Journey Framework

### Step 3 — Offer Presentation
- Present using the value stack from Phase 1B — outcomes first, then features, then price
- Use price anchoring: full standalone value of each element → reveal actual price → guarantee
- The offer name and framing from Phase 1B is the language used here — consistency matters

### Step 4 — Live False Belief Diagnosis

When a buyer hesitates or objects, the words they use are almost never the real reason. Underneath every hesitation is one of four belief failures:

| Belief category | What the buyer is actually saying | Story type to deploy |
|---|---|---|
| New opportunity | "This category / project doesn't work" | Origin / new opportunity story |
| Vehicle | "I don't understand the mechanism — how does this actually produce results?" | Vehicle story (plain-language explanation via a real client) |
| Internal | "I'm not the kind of person who makes this decision" | Internal belief story (a buyer who had the same doubt and closed anyway) |
| External | "Something outside my control will stop this — market, advisor, partner, timing" | External belief story (a buyer who had the exact fear and moved anyway) |

**How to diagnose in the moment:**

1. Hear the hesitation. Don't respond to the words — respond to the category behind them.
2. Ask one question to confirm: "What specifically is making you hesitate?" Then stop talking.
3. Map the answer to a belief category.
4. Deploy the matching story. 60 seconds, plain language, ends at the moment of belief change.
5. Return to the Big Domino after the story, then re-present the offer element they were stuck on.

**What not to do:** Don't counter an objection with logic. Logic against a false belief produces defense, not movement. A story doesn't argue — it just shows someone else making the same journey. The buyer does the rest themselves.

All four story types are built in Phase 1I and stored in the client's playbook. If a new objection type surfaces repeatedly, add a story for it.

### Step 5 — Committed Next Step
- Every conversation ends with a committed next step — never "I'll think about it"
- Accepted next steps: signed LOI, deposit paid, property visit booked, second call scheduled with a date
- A buyer who won't commit to a next step is not a qualified buyer

### Step 5 — Follow-Up
- All follow-ups are tracked in CRM with a date and action
- Sequence continues until yes, no, or disqualified — never goes cold
